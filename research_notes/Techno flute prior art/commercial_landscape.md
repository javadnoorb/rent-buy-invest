# Commercial and Maker Landscape: 3D-Printed Instruments and Acoustic Music Toys

Method note (read first): this research ran on 2026-11-21 in a sandbox where the session could not open most pages directly. makerworld.com, printables.com, inc.com, ugearsmodels.com, toyassociation.org and sites.google.com all failed to resolve. Most findings below come from search-engine result summaries of the cited URLs, not from reading the full pages. Check prices, download counts and dollar totals on the live pages before relying on them. Names the brief suggested ("Glaesel", "Robert Hyde", "Lucas Gleaner") did NOT appear in any result, so they are not reported.

## 1. Sellers of 3D-printed flutes, ocarinas, whistles, didgeridoos, recorders, and typical prices

### Takeaway
Finished 3D-printed wind instruments sell mostly as cheap print-to-order items. Ocarinas on Etsy run about $7–25, with premium resin instruments around $99. For flutes, whistles and didgeridoos, the visible market is mostly free or paid design files rather than branded makers of physical products. No dedicated 3D-printed didgeridoo retailer was found.

### Cited Findings
- Etsy has many 3D-printed 12-hole ocarinas priced from $6.99 to $25.00, most of them $15–25 (search summary of Etsy listing results) — [Etsy listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1011243515); [Etsy shop DINDADA](https://www.etsy.com/shop/DINDADA)
- GoImagine lists a 3D-printed playable 12-hole ocarina at $18.70. It is printed to order, with no cancellations — [GoImagine](https://goimagine.com/12-hole-ocarina-playable-ocarina-ocarina-of-time-ocarina-flute-zelda-ocarina-link-ocarina-ocarina-instrument-3d-printed)
- STL Ocarina sells a "12 Hole 3D Printed Resin Tenor Ocarina in F Major – The Future of Sound" at $99.00. This is a premium price point for a printed instrument — [stlocarina.com](https://www.stlocarina.com/collections/classic-12-hole-ocarinas/products/12-hole-3d-printed-resin-tenor-ocarina-in-f-major-the-future-of-sound)
- King Custom Products (Indiemade) sells 12-hole ocarinas with custom boxes, which is a gift-packaging upsell — [Indiemade](https://kingcustomproducts.indiemade.com/comment/291)
- Price baseline: mass-produced ceramic and plastic ocarinas cost about $7–20 at Walmart — [Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/18376555543); a pricing guide is at [Instruments du Monde](https://instruments-du-monde.com/en/blogs/ocarina/how-much-ocarina-cost-price)
- Whistles: the Chiff & Fipple forum has threads on 3D-printed whistles. One poster describes a 2019 attempt to have a 3D-printed whistle made that "ran into a lot of issues", which suggests quality is still a hurdle for printed whistles — [Chiff & Fipple thread](https://www.chiffandfipple.com/t/3d-printed-whistles-take-2/106296); [second thread](https://www.chiffandfipple.com/t/3d-printed-whistle/100323)
- A 3D-printed baroque recorder project was covered by the trade press — [3DPrint.com](https://3dprint.com/88386/3d-printed-baroque-recorder/)
- Didgeridoo: no finished 3D-printed didgeridoo for sale turned up. Designs found: "Modular Didgeridoo" on Printables, mirrored on Thangs with licence restrictions, and "Minidoo" on MakerWorld, whose licence bans selling digital or printed copies — [Printables](https://www.printables.com/model/565875-modular-didgeridoo); [Thangs](https://thangs.com/m/889151); [MakerWorld](https://makerworld.com/en/models/13826)
- Spirit Gallery (Australia) lists a "CrookedStixz and 3D Didgeridoos Collaboration" didgeridoo. The search summary does not confirm that "3D Didgeridoos" means 3D-printed — [Spirit Gallery AH423](https://www.spiritgallery.com.au/AH423)
- Synthetic (non-printed) didgeridoo competitors include the Meinl Synthetic S-Shape and Pro Synthetic, sold at Guitar Center and Thomann — [Guitar Center](https://www.guitarcenter.com/MEINL/Synthetic-S-Shape-Didgeridoo.gc); [Thomann](https://thomannmusic.com/meinl_pro_synthetic_didgeridoo.htm)
- Etsy flute shops found (SolarFlutes, a large bass flute maker; wowflutes/joecow, about 150 sales) appear to make conventional PVC or wood flutes, not printed ones — [SolarFlutes](https://www.etsy.com/ie/shop/SolarFlutes); [joecow](https://www.etsy.com/shop/joecow)

### Inferences
- Printed ocarinas are a commodity under $25, and much of that demand rides on unlicensed "Zelda/Ocarina of Time" keywords, which is an IP risk. A techno flute probably needs to be sold as a novel, premium object (roughly $40–100, like the $99 resin ocarina) rather than competing at commodity prices.
- No one appears to sell a polyphonic, multi-pipe "techno" wind instrument as a finished product, so the niche looks open. This is not exhaustively verified.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Etsy sales counts, review numbers or current prices directly, because the pages were not fetchable.
- The suggested names (Glaesel, Robert Hyde, Lucas Gleaner) are unverified and should not be cited.
- Found no established "3D-printed flute brand" with disclosed revenue.

## 2. Instrument models on Printables, Thingiverse, Cults3D, MyMiniFactory and MakerWorld: paid vs free, downloads, commercial licences

### Takeaway
Printable instruments (pan flutes, whistles, recorders, ocarinas, didgeridoos) are plentiful and mostly free. Some designers use free models as a funnel to paid variants in their own shop. Commercial rights are set per design and are usually withheld, or sold through creator memberships (MyMiniFactory). Download counts could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Free pan-flute designs on Printables include "Daphnis – A 3D Printable Pan Flute v1.0", "Pan Flute", "Chromatic Pan Flute 7 octave customisable" and "Hexa Pan Flutes 24 variations of keyscale" — [Daphnis](https://www.printables.com/model/439762-daphnis-a-3d-printable-pan-flute-v10); [Pan flute](https://www.printables.com/model/439832-pan-flute); [Chromatic](https://www.printables.com/model/442532-chromatic-pan-flute-7-octave-customisable); [Hexa](https://www.printables.com/model/1437235-hexa-pan-flutes-24-variations-of-keyscale)
- MakerWorld model 471686 is a twisted F# major flute that "plays similarly to a Native American flute or penny whistle" and prints flat without supports. The designer offers other sizes "as well as a drone version… in my shop", which is a free-to-paid funnel and a drone-flute precedent — [MakerWorld 471686](https://makerworld.com/en/models/471686)
- MakerWorld "ModueFlute" is a modular whistle-flute "designed for sound experimentation". The same designer makes a TriTone Slider Whistle with interchangeable attachments — [MakerWorld 1148759](https://makerworld.com/models/1148759)
- Other instrument designs: a MakerWorld pan flute with threaded plugs, a MakerWorld tin whistle, and free recorder designs on MyMiniFactory — [MakerWorld 1149246](https://www.makerworld.com/en/models/1149246); [Tin whistle](https://makerworld.com/ja/models/1148967-tin-whistle); [MyMiniFactory recorder](https://www.myminifactory.com/object/26916); [MyMiniFactory recorder 2](https://www.myminifactory.com/object/3d-print-105583)
- Curated roundups of printable instruments: All3DP and the Prusa blog (recorder, ocarina, kazoo). An All3DP ocarina roundup features an ocarina by "Abmet" on Thingiverse — [All3DP instruments](https://all3dp.com/2/3d-printed-instruments-music/); [Prusa blog](https://blog.prusa3d.com/3d-print-a-recorder-an-ocarina-or-a-kazoo-3d-pritned-musical-instruments_49390/); [All3DP ocarinas](https://all3dp.com/2/3d-printed-ocarina-3d-models/)
- Licensing practice: each file on Thingiverse, Cults, MakerWorld or MyMiniFactory carries a licence. A CC licence with NonCommercial (NC) bars selling prints, while one without NC allows it. On Cults3D the uploader chooses between personal-use-only and permission to sell prints — [All3DP legal guide](https://all3dp.com/1/things-to-3d-print-and-sell-a-legal-guide/)
- MyMiniFactory sells commercial rights mainly through creators' monthly "Commercial License Memberships" — [All3DP legal guide](https://all3dp.com/1/things-to-3d-print-and-sell-a-legal-guide/)
- MakerWorld licences never allow selling, transferring or sharing the digital files themselves (STL) — [All3DP legal guide](https://all3dp.com/1/things-to-3d-print-and-sell-a-legal-guide/)
- Platform commission, from a secondary blog (unverified): Cults3D takes 20% on paid files, and MyMiniFactory takes 10–15% depending on creator tier — [Printworthy blog](https://tsetsobg.github.io/printworthy/articles/where-to-sell-stl-files.html); a vendor page also notes payment-processing fees and a MyMiniFactory payout minimum — [PayRequest](https://payrequest.io/en/products/digital-downloads/3d-printing-files)
- Direct-to-creator file selling, for example through Kajabi, is promoted as an alternative to marketplaces — [Kajabi blog](https://kajabi.com/blog/sell-3d-print-files); [Shapr3D](https://www.shapr3d.com/content-library/sell-3d-print-files)

### Inferences
- A free "lite" model (for example a single drone pipe) on Printables or MakerWorld, upselling a paid full techno flute file or a finished print, matches what instrument designers already do.
- Free designs are everywhere, so file revenue alone is likely small. Treat files as marketing and earn on physical units, kits or licences.
- An NC licence (or a MakerWorld-style no-resale licence) protects physical-product sales. A paid commercial tier for print farms is a possible extra revenue line.

### Gaps
- Download and like counts for instrument models could not be read (the pages were blocked). No reliable figures for what paid instrument files earn.
- Thingiverse instrument download data was not retrieved.

## 3. Kickstarter/Indiegogo campaigns for acoustic or mechanical music toys and kits

### Takeaway
Mechanical and acoustic music kits have a proven crowdfunding track record. The Ugears Hurdy-Gurdy (a hand-cranked wooden kit) raised about $288–300k from 2,954 backers against a $15k goal, and the Muro Box N40 was fully funded within hours. Teenage Engineering's flat-pack "Pocket Operator modular" kits ($149–499) show demand for self-assembly music hardware, though they ran into manufacturing trouble.

### Cited Findings
- Ugears Hurdy-Gurdy (Kickstarter, July 2017, the company's fourth campaign) had 2,954 backers from 5 continents — [Ugears blog](https://ugearsmodels.com/blogs/main-blog/ugears-successfully-completed-the-hurdy-gurdy-campaign-the-4th-campaign-on-kickstarter)
- It raised "nearly $300,000" against a $15,000 goal according to UADN, and $288,326 according to Habr. The sources differ slightly — [UADN](https://uadn.net/?p=35187); [Habr](https://habr.com/ru/post/407303)
- An earlier Ugears range crowdfunded $74,000 — [Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/technology/ukrainian-startup-ugears-crowdfunds-74000-new-range-wooden-models.html)
- Ugears also ran a BackerKit campaign, "Ugears Games: unique mechanical devices for tabletop" — [BackerKit](https://www.backerkit.com/projects/978262034/ugears-games-unique-mechanical-devices-for-tableto)
- Hurdy-Gurdy kit coverage: [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/ugears-hurdy-gurdy-kit/50231); [Laughing Squid](https://laughingsquid.com/hurdy-gurdy-mechanical-model/)
- Muro Box N40 (an app- and MIDI-programmable mechanical music box, not hand-cranked) had a $110,359 goal and was 100% funded within the first hours. It was later sold through Indiegogo and pre-orders — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/music/muro-box-n40-kickstarter/?amp=true); [Indiegogo](https://indiegogo.com/projects/muro-box-n40-programmable-mechanical-music-box/pies); [Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2023/10/28/muro-box-n40-chromatic-midi-music-box-now-available-to-pre-order/)

Their results were not verified — [PledgeBox](https://www.pledgebox.com/projects/lunar-witch-a-magical-wooden-music-box); [Kicktraq Gecho](https://www.kicktraq.com/projects/967276512/gecho-loopsynth-a-modern-equivalent-of-the-music-b/); [Kicktraq Music Boxes](https://www.kicktraq.com/projects/806377992/music-boxes-for-the-world/); [BackerKit Rhythmo](https://www.backerkit.com/projects/rhythmo/beatbox-by-rhythmo-diy-cardboard-midi-controller-kit); [BackerKit glo](https://www.backerkit.com/projects/phonicbloom/glo-is-a-fun-interactive-and-meditative-sound-expe)

## 4. Toy-industry licensing from independent inventors: royalties, pitching, and regulation (CPSIA, ASTM F963, EN71)

### Takeaway
Inventor royalties usually run about 3–7% of net sales (around 5% is typical, and above 10% is rare). Licensed-character products pay inventors less, around 2–3%. Hasbro takes outside ideas through its SPARK portal plus established professional-inventor relationships. In the US, a product intended mainly for children aged 12 and under triggers CPSIA third-party testing to ASTM F963, with only partial Small Batch relief. In the EU, the Toy Safety Directive covers products for under-14s, and a "real musical instrument" can fall outside it on design criteria.

### Cited Findings — royalties and pitching
- Most royalty rates fall between 3 and 7 percent, and rates above 10 percent are "extremely rare" (Stephen Key, inventor-licensing author) — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/stephen-key/how-to-get-a-higher-royalty-rate-for-your-invention.html); [InventRight](https://inventright.com/blog/how-to-tell-what-your-invention-is-worth)
- Rates can range from under 1% to over 30%, but most hover around 5%. A larger upfront payment generally means a lower royalty — [EPO Inventors' Handbook](https://www.epo.org/learning/materials/inventors-handbook/dealing-with-companies/royalties.html); [WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/ip-commercialization/w/blog/how-to-set-intellectual-property-royalty-rates-in-a-licensing-agreement)
- A legal overview puts toy licensing royalties at 5–15% for established brands. This is general, not a toy-industry survey — [UpCounsel](https://upcounsel.com/toy-licensing-agreements)
- A toy-agent page says an inventor's royalty may fall to 2–3% when the product also carries a licensed character, because character fees take part of the margin (search summary; the page could not be opened) — [Better Managed Toys](https://sites.google.com/view/better-managed-toys/for-curious-minds)
- Licensing International publishes a licensing primer for inventors — [Licensing International](https://licensinginternational.org/?p=37179)
- Hasbro does not accept unsolicited concepts outside the SPARK Hasbro portal. SPARK runs alongside its professional-inventor relationships — [Hasbro SPARK steps](https://spark.hasbro.com/submit/steps); [Hasbro IP form](https://docs.hasbro.com/upload/legal/intellectualpropertyform.pdf)
- Per Toy Book (2019), anyone in the US over 18 can submit through SPARK — [Toy Book via Issuu](https://www.issuu.com/toybook/docs/6-19/30)
- Hasbro's product acquisitions department meets regularly with outside inventors (from a lawsuit write-up) — [Bloomberg 2016](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2016-toy-inventor-lawsuit); [Wexler v. Hasbro docket](https://www.docketalarm.com/cases/New_York_Southern_District_Court/1--20-cv-01100/Wexler_v._Hasbro_Inc/1)
- InventRight says inventors can now take ideas to Hasbro directly. InventRight sells inventor services, so it has a commercial interest — [InventRight](https://inventright.com/blog/how-hasbro-finds-its-next-great-ideas)
- Stephen Key's guide on licensing to toy companies (could not be opened) — [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/stephen-key/how-to-license-an-idea-to-a-toy-company.html)

### Cited Findings — US regulation
- CPSIA Section 106 made ASTM F963 a mandatory standard. Toys designed or intended primarily for children aged 12 and under need third-party testing by a CPSC-accepted lab and a Children's Product Certificate (CPC) — [CPSC Toy Safety](https://cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Toy-Safety); [CPSC CPC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Childrens-Product-Certificate-CPC/); [Rules requiring 3rd-party testing](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Testing-Certification/Lab-Accreditation/Rules-Requiring-Third-Party-Testing)
- Small Batch Manufacturers must register with CPSC every year to get relief. They must ALWAYS third-party test for "Group A" rules. For "Group B" rules they can certify on other reasonable testing. Relief "is not an exemption" from the requirements themselves — [CPSC Small Batch FAQ](https://cpsc.gov/FAQ/Small-Batch); [CPSC Small Batch page](https://cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Small-Business-Resources/Small-Batch-Manufacturers-and-Third-Party-); [SaferProducts registration](https://www.saferproducts.gov/BusinessRegister/SmallBatchManufacturer)
- CPSC lists the ASTM F963 sections and their testing requirements in one chart, which is the place to check each section's group — [CPSC ASTM F963 chart](https://cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Toy-Safety/ASTM-F-963-Chart)
- CPSC-accepted labs include a test scope for "Sound Producing Toys" (ASTM F963 §4.5, 16 CFR 1250), which is directly relevant to a whistle or flute toy — [CPSC lab search](https://cpsc.gov/labsearch)
- At minimum, the first production batch must be third-party tested at a CPSC-accepted lab — [CPSC Initial Testing FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Initial-Testing)
- CPSC has guidance for online sellers — [CPSC Online Sellers FAQ](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Online-Sellers-Safety-Guide-FAQs); [Business Desktop Reference Guide 2023](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/CPSC-Business-Desktop-Reference-Guide-Sep-2023.pdf)

### Cited Findings — EU regulation
- The EU Toy Safety Directive 2009/48/EC (EN71) covers products with play value designed for children under 14. Toy musical instruments are in scope — [ComplianceGate product list](https://www.compliancegate.com/toy-safety-directive-en-71-product-list/); [ComplianceGate guide](https://www.compliancegate.com/toy-safety-directive/)
- European Commission guidance separates toy instruments from "real" instruments using design and materials (chosen for sound quality), level of detail, realistic dimensions, accompanying documents, place of sale, intended audience and price. Toys tend to be scaled-down, more colourful, or have fewer keys — [EC guidance document](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/34204/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/native); [TÜV SÜD on 2019 update](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-in/e-ssentials-newsletter/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/e-ssentials-4-2019/eu-toy-guidance-document-for-musical-instruments-updated)
- The directive's Annex I exclusion for "products for collectors" requires a visible "intended for collectors of 14 years and above" label. This is a collectors' carve-out, not an instrument exemption — [legislation.gov.uk Annex I](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2009/48/annex/I/data.pdf)

### Inferences
- Selling the techno flute as an adult or "14+" real instrument (detailed, sound-optimised design, music-retail positioning, no child-appeal styling) probably avoids CPSIA children's-product testing and EN71 toy scope. An age label alone does not settle it: CPSC and the EC look at the whole presentation.
- A kid-targeted version adds lab-testing cost (the first batch at an accepted lab, including sound-producing-toy acoustics) plus a CPC. It also raises FDM-specific questions (small parts, sharp edges, material chemistry) that make one-off print-on-demand harder.
- For licensing, a realistic plan is about 3–5% of net wholesale, plus an advance, through SPARK or through toy agents. That is low per unit, so licensing suits the mass-market kid version better than a niche adult instrument.

### Gaps
- Typical advance amounts and toy-agent commission splits were not verified, because the agent pages could not be fetched.
- No verified data on Mattel's inventor-submission process or Toy Fair inventor programmes.
- Lab-testing costs for ASTM F963 were not found.
- Not verified whether §4.5 sound-producing toys is a Group A or Group B rule.

## 5. Market size signals for music toys and DIY instrument kits

### Takeaway
US toy sales grew 6% in 2025 (Circana), driven by kidults and collectibles. Estimates for "musical toys" are unreliable and range from about $2B to about $9.7B globally depending on definition. No credible figure exists for DIY instrument kits; Ugears and Teenage Engineering are the proxy evidence.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 US toy dollar sales rose 6% year over year: units up 3%, average selling price up 4%. Games & Puzzles was among the three supercategories behind 92% of growth (Circana via Toy Association, Feb 2026) — [Toy Association](https://ems.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/us-toy-industry-returns-to-growth-in-2025-circana-reports.aspx); [Circana](https://www.circana.com/post/u-s-toy-industry-returns-to-growth-in-2025-circana-reports)
- Pokémon sales rose 87% to $2.5B in 2025. Licensed toys made up 37% of first-half 2025 US sales — [Toy Association](https://ems.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/us-toy-industry-returns-to-growth-in-2025-circana-reports.aspx); [Circana H1 2025](https://www.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2025_News/circana-reports-first-half-2025-us-global-toy-industry-performance.aspx)
- Kidults and premium toys drove the rebound — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-toy-industry-2025-growth-kidult-trend-tariff-risks); [Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/premium-toys-drive-us-toy-market-rebound/812849/); [Retail Dive adults](https://www.retaildive.com/news/adults-us-toy-sales-2025/749912/)
- About $30.3B in 2025 US retail toy sales per a third-party blog citing Circana (secondary, verify) — [ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/us-toy-sales-statistics)
- About 80% of toys sold in the US are made in China, which leaves the sector exposed to tariffs — [Toy Association](https://ems.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/us-toy-industry-returns-to-growth-in-2025-circana-reports.aspx)
- Musical toys market estimates (low-quality market-research reports with conflicting definitions):
  - $2.1B (2024) growing to $3.4B (2033) — [DataHorizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/global-musical-toys-market-50971)
  - $9.68B (2025) growing to $14.94B (2032) — [ReportPrime](https://www.reportprime.com/musical-toys-r17470.md)
  - $9.10B (2024) growing to $14.05B (2031) — [Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/musical-toys-market/)
  - "Music sound toys" at $5.15B (2025), with toy pianos/keyboards at 35.8% — [pMarketResearch](https://pmarketresearch.com/worldwide-music-sound-toy-market-research/)
  - Kids' musical toys at about $210M (2024) — [MarketResearch.com/Bosson](https://www.marketresearch.com/Bosson-Research-v4252/Global-Kids-Musical-Toys-Research-40730767)

### Inferences
- The kidult and premium trend favours an adult-positioned techno flute or groove box over a kids' toy, which also matches the regulatory findings above.
- Quote musical-toy market sizes as a range ($2–10B) with a caveat, not as a single figure.

### Gaps
- No credible market size for DIY instrument kits or for 3D-printed instruments specifically.
- No public revenue for Ugears or for Teenage Engineering's PO line.
